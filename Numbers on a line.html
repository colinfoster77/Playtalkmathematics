NUmbers on a line

image to come

What you need

Outdoor chalks, large 0-5 dotty dice (i.e. one face is blank, with no dots)

What to do

Children roll the dice, count - or subitise - the dots and then chalk the correct numeral on the ground. When you think it appropriate, introduce the idea of chalking the numbers in order along a horizontal line, from zero.

What to try next

  • Jumping onto a digit when a number is rolled.
  • Jumping along the line of numbers saying each number in turn, up and back. Can we make up a game to play on our line of numbers?
  • Can they continue the line above 5?
  • Use a 0,1,2 dice to jump along the line of numbers, starting from zero. When you roll zero, you don’t move.
  • Take it in turns to secretly stand on – and hide - a number on the line for others to identify which number is hidden.
  • Build a ‘How old are you?’ number line together, with children, relatives and friends positioned on it. Include zero. Do we know anyone who is zero? What does that mean?
  • Count aloud in unison up from zero (stop at different numbers as experiences develop) and back again. Slap your knees or stamp in a steady rhythm as you count.

What to say

  • You’ve rolled (…). What number shall we write?
  • It’s blank! Nothing! Can we write something for nothing?
  • When I say “go” jump on …
  • Can we jump on the numbers in order?
  • Let’s write them all in order along this line, starting with zero
  • What comes next/after 3? Are you sure?

What might happen

  • Toddlers have practical experience of zero, usually when things have “all gone”, or “nothing left”, this activity introduces ‘nothingness’ and ‘doing nothing’. Some children might insist on rolling the dice again to get a number of dots.
  • Some children might know the symbol for zero, whilst others might be meeting this for the first time. Ask children where they have seen it before and what it might mean.
  • Writing the 0-5 numerals in order on a horizontal line might be challenging enough for some. It is far more challenging to roll the dice to produce each number randomly, as children have to decide where to position the rolled number each time, allowing for numbers not yet there.
  • The numbers might not be evenly spaced, which at this early stage is fine as we are focussing on ordering numerals in sequence.
  • Children might reverse numerals when writing them. As age-appropriate, you can correct this sensitively without stunting children’s enthusiasm.

What matters

  • Here, we are building young children’s number sense through playing with and creating simple number lines – usually a horizontal line on which equally spaced points are given numbers, and including zero. This is a spatial model of number. This is not the same thing as children using printed number lines, which is difficult for young children.
  • Children think about zero in different ways, as an endpoint (“5,4,3,2,1, ZERO!”), or as an absence of something (see activity ‘Same and Different’). Zero may well play a special role in children’s numerical understanding, as they come to learn that it ‘behaves’ in exceptional ways.
  • Building a mental number line – an ordered list of numerals - with increasing sophistication as children become older, is as important for their mathematical development as their experiences of numbers as amounts. Number line understanding contributes to understandings of number relationships, estimation and calculation.
  • Ordering numbers is the process of working out which number is larger than or smaller than another. Ordinal number knowledge is not just about the quite complex ordinal language of “first” “second” “third”… and so on, but also to do with how each number relates to another in the sequence of counting numbers.
  • Playing simple board (track) games can develop children’s ordinal understanding.

What to discuss

  • Are there any surprises as children engage in this activity?
  • How did different children respond when ordering the numerals?
